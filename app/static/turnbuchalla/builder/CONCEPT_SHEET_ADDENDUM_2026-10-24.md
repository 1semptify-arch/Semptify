# Addendum to the Concept Sheet — corrections and clarifications
## Minnesota Rent Assurance & Deposit Protection Act · Turnbuchalla model · 2026-10-24

*This addendum accompanies the two-page concept sheet dated 10/23. It
does not replace it. Four statements in the sheet were tightened on
re-read; the corrected versions are below, with the reason for each.
Reviewers holding the 10/23 sheet should read it with these in hand.*

---

### 1. "Proven elsewhere" — the right precedent is the UK, not France

**Sheet says:** "France runs the national version (Visale); the mechanic
is proven."

**Corrected:** The **United Kingdom** has held every private-sector
tenancy deposit in government-approved custodial schemes since 2007 —
deposits leave the landlord's hands at signing, disputes go to a free
independent adjudication service, and the schemes fund themselves off
the pooled float. That is the deposit title of this Act, running at
national scale for eighteen years.

France's **Visale** proves a different thing — that a national rent
guarantee can operate — but Visale is a surety: when it pays the
landlord, it recovers from the tenant, and it does not cover deposits.
This Act adopts the UK's deposit custody and Visale's rent guarantee and
removes the clawback from both. No existing program does all three.

*Why the change:* citing Visale as proof invites the correction "Visale
claws back from tenants" — which is the exact practice this Act bans.

---

### 2. Docket savings — the honest fraction

**Sheet says:** "A large share of Minnesota's ~20,000–25,000 annual
eviction filings are collections wearing court robes… Claiming those
events through insurance removes them from the docket entirely."

**Corrected:** Roughly **a third to a half** of Minnesota's 23,000–26,000
annual eviction filings are collections-shaped — serial nonpayment
filings and deposit disputes the pool is designed to absorb. Holdover,
lease-breach, and conduct cases remain in court by design. That is
**7,000–12,000 cases a year** off the docket.

*Why the change:* "entirely" and "a large share" are the words a
committee corrects. The scoped number is defensible from Eviction Lab
serial-filing data and Minnesota Judicial Branch filing counts, and it
is still a large number.

---

### 3. Who is covered — "every residential landlord," not "every licensed"

**Sheet says:** "Every licensed residential landlord in Minnesota…"

**Corrected:** "Every residential landlord in Minnesota, as defined in
the Act." Minnesota has no statewide rental license; licensing is
municipal and uneven. Coverage under the Act attaches to the rental
relationship itself, not to a city license.

*Why the change:* "licensed by whom?" is the first drafting question a
staff attorney asks, and the answer today is "it depends on the city."

---

### 4. What the landlord who never files actually gets

**Sheet says** (under "Who pays"): landlords fund a matched share and
clean books earn a no-claim dividend.

**Added:** Most landlords never file an eviction and never fight a
deposit claim. For them the matched share buys four things they pay for
today in other forms:

- **Independent claims adjudication** — the dispute process that costs
  $500–$2,000 per contested deposit elsewhere, at no per-case charge.
- **§504B.178 exposure off their books** — the deposit itemization and
  21-day rules are the most-violated provisions in Minnesota
  landlord-tenant practice; under custodial escrow the landlord no
  longer holds, itemizes, or returns the deposit.
- **A claims-history record** that replaces paid tenant-screening
  products with the pool's own data.
- **The no-claim dividend** — clean-book years return a share of the
  matched premium from pool surplus.

*Why the addition:* the sheet argues averages (~$7,000 per avoided
contested eviction); the landlord lobby argues medians. This is the
median landlord's answer.

---

### A note on comparisons other states

New Jersey is piloting deposit **assistance** (A1925, 2026 session — a
state grant covering a low-income household's deposit in eight
counties). The UK runs deposit **custody**. France runs rent
**guarantee** with clawback. This Act is the first proposal to combine
custody, guarantee, and no clawback in one statute. Minnesota would be
first, not a copy.
