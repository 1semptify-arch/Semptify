*Written for the 3:00 deadline. Read as a stranger would: a House member's
staffer, Birnberg, and the landlord lobby. Rationale stated for every

---

## The one-paragraph verdict

The bill is good — the *fairness design* is better than anything the
SDR industry or the French model offers, and the no-subrogation clause
is exactly the right hill. But it is **three bills wearing one coat**,
and the coat is sized for the weakest of the three. The deposit-escrow
title is proven (UK, since 2007), self-funding, and cheap on a fiscal
note. The hardship-rent title is unprecedented in the US, unpriced, and
is the piece a staff attorney will stall on. Right now the packet makes
the strong piece carry the weak piece's risk. **Finalize by re-ordering,
not by adding: lead with deposits, pilot the rent guarantee, park
belongings.** Three sentences in the concept sheet still overclaim and
must be fixed before anyone else reads it (listed below).

## SWOT

### Strengths
- **No subrogation, statute-locked.** The single clause that separates
  insurance from the Rhino/Jetty structure — and the Jetty SDNY suit
  (filed Oct 6) makes it the headline, not a footnote. Verified 10/18.
- **The fairness axiom is real design, not a slogan.** Tenant-side abuse
  controls are as concrete as landlord-side penalties. Tenant-side
  reviewers (HOME Line, NHLP) will notice that; most citizen bills don't
  have it.
- **Load-bearing precedents exist and run at scale.** UK custodial TDP
  (deposits held for the tenancy, free adjudication, funded off float)
  and federal APTC (monthly advance of an annual credit, reconciled on
  the return). Nothing in the deposit title requires inventing machinery.
- **The 10/23 audit→remediation pass.** Six Tier-1 holes found and
  closed in one day with named precedents. That rigor is itself a
  credential when a staffer asks "has anyone stress-tested this?"
- **Bipartisan two-lane frame** — consumer protection / court-cost
  savings — and a **pilot fallback at MHFA that needs no bill.** The
  project can't fully fail.
- **The home-state hook.** §504B.178 (deposit statute) is the #1
  violation source in landlord-tenant practice; custodial escrow is a
  natural amendment to a statute Birnberg wrote.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope.** Rent guarantee + deposit escrow + belongings insurance +
  claims registry + new adjudication body + a DOR payment build. The
  hole audit said it (H12, PASSAGE #6). Committees kill complexity, and
  every added leg hands the fiscal note another unknown.
- **The hardship-rent leg is the riskiest piece and it's welded to the
  safest.** "Is hardship insurable?" (H9) has no US precedent to point
  at — UI is the frame, but UI is payroll-funded and federally
  backstopped. If an actuary prices it high, "self-funding" dies and the
  deposit title dies with it unless it stands alone.
- **No actuary has touched any number.** Everything from $184/yr base
  premium to the 25% admin load is modeled, not measured. The concept
  sheet says "designed to pay for itself" — honest wording, but a
  staffer hears "unpriced."
- **M1PR reach.** "Nothing new leaves a pocket" is true only for credit
  claimants (~420–450K of ~600K renter households, H2). The sheet
  discloses the over-cap case but not the non-claimant gap.
- **Statute text doesn't exist yet.** BILL_LIVE is a clause list and
  open questions, not drafted sections. Fine for Birnberg; not fine
  for a member's office, who will ask "where's the bill?"
- **Sponsor credibility.** Pre-501(c), founder is a pro se litigant
  against a landlord. Mitigated by the "case = exhibit only if asked"
  rule and Birnberg's read — but it is the first thing opposition will
  say out loud.

### Opportunities
- **Split into titles, pass the strong one first.**
  *Title I — Deposit Protection* (custodial escrow + independent
  adjudication + no-subrogation + registry): proven, self-funding off
  float, trivial fiscal note, directly amends 504B.178, absorbs the
  deposit half of Conciliation Court's housing docket. *Title II — Rent
  Assurance*: authorized as an MHFA pilot on LIHTC stock with a report
  date; full rollout contingent on pilot actuarials. *Title III —
  Belongings*: opt-in, deferred. Same bill, same subject ("residential
  rental risk"), but each title survives the others' failure.
- **Federal tailwind is live right now:** Blumenthal→FTC letter (Oct 8
  2026), FTC rental-fee rulemaking (Mar 2026), NCLC "Tenant Insecurity"
  (May 2026), NHLP's SDR framework whose fix-list is a subset of this
  bill. Quote them; don't paraphrase.
- **Convert the SDR industry instead of fighting it (H10):** licensed
  SDRs may sell in MN only as no-subrogation, refundable-principal,
  registry-reporting products. Gives Rhino/Obligo a lane, splits their
  lobby, and makes the bill "regulation," not "prohibition."
- **The median-landlord value stack (H6)** is written in REMEDIATION but
  not yet in the concept sheet as a list. One bullet converts the
  "it's a tax" attack into "it's a product you already pay more for."

### Threats
- **MMHA** testifies on every housing bill; mandatory participation +
  deposits leaving landlord hands = two threats to one lobby.
- **SDR national campaign** ("Renters Choice," Rhino-funded) is an
  organized, funded opponent with a bipartisan mayors' letter behind it.
- **Fiscal note sticker shock** — a contingent state backstop shows up
  as a liability line regardless of how it's framed.
- **Calendar.** 2027 session pre-filing is Nov–Dec 2026 — that is now.
  No carrier = no bill; Port isn't running again; Birnberg hasn't read
  it yet.
- **"The state takes your deposit."** Legally wrong (tenant property in
  bonded escrow), rhetorically sticky, and the SHARE copy's "Deposits
  move into bonded escrow… and start paying you" is the right answer —
  keep saying it that way.

These are overclaims that survived the 10/23 pass. Each one is a
credibility wound a staffer can inflict in one sentence.

|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | "Proven elsewhere — France runs the national version (Visale); the mechanic is proven." | DECISIONS 10/23 already found Visale is a **surety with tenant clawback** and does **not** cover deposits — i.e., it's the thing this bill bans. The sheet still cites it as proof. | Replace with UK TDP: "The UK has held every private deposit in custodial escrow with free independent adjudication since 2007 — funded off the float. France's Visale proves the rent-guarantee mechanic but claws back from the tenant; this Act doesn't." |
| D2 | "A large share of Minnesota's ~20,000–25,000 annual eviction filings are collections wearing court robes… removes them from the docket entirely." | H11 fix never landed. "Entirely" and "a large share" are the exact words that get corrected in committee. | "Roughly a third to half of filings are collections-shaped — serial nonpayment filings and deposit disputes the pool absorbs; holdover, breach, and conduct cases stay in court by design. That is 7–12K cases a year off the docket." |

## The New Jersey question — honest answer

Brad: "it's in New Jersey, I think it's a good thing." I checked. What
New Jersey actually has:

- **A1925 (2026 session, Quijano/Rowan):** a *Security Deposit Assistance
  Pilot* — the state grants low-income households the cash for a deposit
  in eight counties, $400K appropriation. That is a **subsidy**, not a
  pool, not insurance, no escrow reform. Different model.
- **Gov. Murphy's 2020 pandemic executive order:** let tenants apply
  their existing deposit toward rent. Temporary, expired, no mechanism.
- NJ's deposit statute (N.J.S.A. 46:8-19) requires landlords to hold
  deposits in interest-bearing accounts — closer to MN's 504B.178 than
  to custodial escrow.

**Do not say "New Jersey does this" at 3:00.** Nothing in NJ runs a
pooled, no-subrogation rent-and-deposit guarantee. The honest line is:
"New Jersey is piloting deposit *assistance*; the UK runs deposit
*custody*; France runs rent *guarantee* with clawback. This Act is the
first to put the three together without the clawback." That's a stronger
sentence anyway — it makes Minnesota first, not a copy.

## Possible improvements (beyond the fixes)

- **Title structure in BILL_LIVE** — reorganize the eight locked clauses
  under Title I / II / III so the "pilot the rent leg" option is visible
  in the skeleton, not just in conversation.
- **Rename the hardship leg's frame** from "insurance" to "guarantee
  fund administered like insurance" (H9) everywhere — it answers the
  Commerce-vs-MHFA question and the insurability question in one move.
- **Sunset: 5-year with mandatory year-3 report** instead of a 3-year
  hard sunset (Tier 3) — stops telling the administrator not to build
  for year 4.
- **Claims-history follows the unit, not the portfolio** (H13) — one
  line, closes the small-landlord adverse-selection lane.
- **Drop the case footer** the moment the sheet goes anywhere other
  than Birnberg (already noted in DRAFTING NOTE — just don't forget).
