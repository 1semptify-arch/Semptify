## What this draft is — and is not

Title I is the proven, self-funding core: it moves every residential
security deposit into bonded escrow for the length of the tenancy,
replaces the landlord's itemized return with independent administrative
adjudication, and locks the no-subrogation clause that separates this
from the deposit-insurance products tenants are already being hurt by.

It does **not** contain: the rent-assurance pool and its funding braid
(Title II), belongings coverage (Title III), or the governing board,
public claims registry, manufactured-claim penalties, home agency, and
sunset (Title IV — though this draft flags every place Title I depends
on them).

## Drafting conventions

- Bracketed `[TBD]` marks = Revisor/counsel completion (section numbers
  for provisions that live elsewhere in the Act, the program
  administrator's identity, time windows still open in BILL_LIVE).
- Subdivision numbering (1a, 1b, 1c, 3a, 3b, 3c) is provisional — the
  Revisor renumbers on insertion; the grouping is what matters.
- "This section" = § 504B.178 as amended. "This Act" = the whole bill.
- Existing subdivisions that must change are handled two ways: full
  replacement text where the change is the custody flip itself
  (subds. 2, 3, 9), and a conforming-amendments table for the rest.

## THE AMENDATORY TEXT

**Section 1.** Minnesota Statutes 2026, section 504B.178, is amended by
adding subdivisions to read:

**Subd. 1a. Definitions.**

(a) As used in this section and sections [TBD — program provisions
elsewhere in this Act]:

(1) "Adjudication body" means the independent administrative body
designated under section [TBD] to decide claims arising under this
section.

(2) "Claim" means a demand for payment from a security deposit under
this section or from the residential rental protection fund under
section [TBD — Title II provisions].

(3) "Escrow agent" means a bank, trust company, or other depository
holding security deposits under contract with the program
administrator and meeting the bonding requirement of paragraph (b).

(4) "Net float" means earnings on pooled security deposits remaining
after (i) the interest payable to tenants under subdivision 1c and
(ii) reasonable costs of escrow and program administration.

(5) "Program" means the residential rental protection program
established under this Act.

(6) "Program administrator" means [the entity designated under
section TBD — proposed home agency: Minnesota Housing Finance Agency,
per BILL_V2 IV-2; NEEDS BRAD confirm].

(7) "Security deposit replacement product" means a product or service
offered to a residential landlord or tenant in substitution for or in
lieu of a security deposit, including a surety bond, guarantee, letter
of credit, or insurance product.

(b) An escrow agent shall maintain a surety bond or deposit insurance
in an amount not less than the pooled security deposits under its
control.

**Subd. 1b. Custody of security deposits; transmission to escrow.**

(a) A security deposit governed by this section is not the property of
the landlord. A landlord who receives a security deposit shall transmit
the deposit to the escrow agent within 14 days after the landlord's
receipt of the deposit. The landlord holds no right, title, or
interest in the deposit other than the right to payment of a claim
allowed under subdivision 3a.

(b) A security deposit in escrow remains in escrow for the duration of
the tenancy and is disbursed only as provided in subdivision 3a.

(c) Upon termination of the landlord's interest in the premises,
whether by sale, assignment, death, appointment of receiver, or
otherwise, the deposit remains in escrow and this section continues to
apply to the tenancy. [Conforming treatment for subds. 5–6 below — the
escrow obligation runs with the tenancy, not the landlord, which
retires the deposit-transfer machinery entirely.]

**Subd. 1c. Interest; net float; claims-free dividend.**

(a) Each security deposit in escrow shall bear simple, noncompounded
interest at the rate of one percent per annum, computed from the first
day of the month following the escrow agent's receipt of the deposit
to the last day of the month in which the deposit is disbursed or
judgment is entered in any civil action involving liability for the
deposit, whichever is earlier. Interest is payable to the tenant. Any
interest amount less than $1 is excluded.

(b) The escrow agent shall invest pooled security deposits in
obligations of the United States or other instruments authorized by
the program administrator. Earnings on pooled deposits in excess of
the tenant interest under paragraph (a) and reasonable costs are net
float and are retained by the program for administration, claims
payment, and adjudication.

(c) The program administrator may declare an annual dividend to a
tenant whose deposit remained in escrow throughout the preceding year
without a paid claim, paid from pool surplus. A dividend is declared,
not guaranteed. [DECISIONS 10/23 — mutual model; supersedes the
10/19 "refund after clean year." Amount/formula = actuarial TBD.]

**Subd. 3a. Claims; adjudication.**

(a) A landlord seeking to withhold all or part of a security deposit,
or a tenant seeking return of a security deposit or payment of a claim
under this section, shall file a claim with the adjudication body.

(b) A landlord claim must be filed:

(1) within 30 days after termination of the tenancy; or

(2) within 10 days after the tenant leaves the building or dwelling due
to the legal condemnation of the building or dwelling in which the
tenant lives for reasons not due to willful, malicious, or
irresponsible conduct of the tenant.

[Windows must accommodate the § 504B.182 inspection timeline —
confirm against Revisor's rules; adjustable. Mirrors the two-prong
structure of existing subd. 3(a).]

(c) A tenant claim may be filed within one year after termination of
the tenancy. [TBD — window open in BILL_LIVE.]

(d) If the landlord does not file a claim within the time allowed by
paragraph (b), the escrow agent shall return the deposit and accrued
interest to the tenant. This paragraph is the tenant-protection core:
no claim filed, deposit comes home.

(e) The adjudication body shall provide the parties notice, an
opportunity to be heard, and a written decision. In any claim, the
burden of proving, by a fair preponderance of the evidence, the reason
for withholding all or any portion of the deposit is on the landlord.
[Preserves existing subd. 3(c) burden allocation verbatim in effect.]

(f) A landlord may withhold or recover from a deposit only amounts
reasonably necessary: (1) to remedy tenant defaults in the payment of
rent or of other funds due to the landlord pursuant to an agreement;
or (2) to restore the premises to their condition at the commencement
of the tenancy, ordinary wear and tear excepted. [Carries forward
existing subd. 3(b) limits into the claims context.]

(g) An appeal from an adjudication decision may be brought in the
district court in the county where the rental property is located or,
at the option of the tenant, in the county of the landlord's
residence. [Preserves the tenant's venue option from existing subd.
9.] The standard of review is [TBD — see counsel question 3].

(h) No action for the recovery of a security deposit may be brought in
conciliation court or in district court, except an appeal under
paragraph (g). [Counsel question 1 — the displacement language.]

**Subd. 3b. Prohibition on subrogation and recourse.**

(a) Payment of a claim under this section, or under any security
deposit replacement product or rental-risk guarantee program, extinguishes
any right of the payor, the landlord, or any other person to recover
the amount paid from the tenant, including by subrogation, assignment,
indemnity, or any other right of recovery.

(b) A contract term providing for subrogation or recourse against a
tenant for a paid claim is void and unenforceable.

(c) A person that seeks recovery from a tenant of an amount paid or
payable under this section or a security deposit replacement product
violates this subdivision and is subject to the penalties of section
[TBD — manufactured-claim penalties, Title IV / clause 5].

**Subd. 3c. Security deposit replacement products.**

(a) A security deposit replacement product may be offered in
connection with a residential rental agreement only if the product:

(1) provides no right of recovery against the tenant following payment
of a claim;

(2) returns to the tenant, at termination of the tenancy, prepaid
principal not applied to paid claims; and

(3) reports claims and outcomes to the public claims registry
established under section [TBD — clause 6, Title IV].

(b) A security deposit replacement product that does not satisfy all
of paragraph (a) is unlawful. The [attorney general / program
administrator — TBD] may seek an injunction and civil penalties
against a person offering or administering a nonconforming product.
[REMEDIATION H10 — convert the SDR industry or exclude it; this is the
convert-or-exclude lane made statutory.]

## SKELETON PROVISIONS THE ACT MUST SUPPLY ELSEWHERE

Title I references entities this amendment does not create. Before this
goes to the Revisor, the Act needs at least skeleton language for:

1. **The program and home agency** — BILL_V2 IV-2 proposes MHFA as fund
   home, independent adjudication body, contracted licensed-insurer
   administration (SFM precedent). NEEDS BRAD confirm.
2. **Program administrator designation** — who contracts escrow agents,
   declares dividends, refers violations.
3. **Escrow agent authorization + bonding rules** — who may hold
   deposits, bond amounts, exam/oversight.
4. **Adjudication body** — establishment, membership (tenant/consumer
   seats per locked clause 4), procedures, funding source (net float +
   admin load per I-8 — adjudication carries its own budget line, not
   buried in admin).
5. **Public claims registry** — locked clause 6; FCRA-modeled accuracy
   and dispute rules (Tier-3 privacy note).
6. **Manufactured-claim penalties** — locked clause 5; surcharge +
   referral, symmetric false-referral penalty, tenant notice/defense
   window (Brad 10/19 referral-abuse controls).
7. **Sunset + year-3 report** — locked clause 7; V2 proposes 5-year term
   (NEEDS BRAD).

Without these, subds. 1a–3c point at undefined terms — flagged so the
Revisor's office sees the dependency, not a hole.

## QUESTIONS FOR COUNSEL (the 504B author)

1. **Displacement.** Does routing every deposit claim through
   administrative adjudication with district-court review cleanly
   displace the conciliation-court and original district-court deposit
   actions under existing subd. 9 — without breaking other lease
   remedies that share the chapter (possession actions, rent escrow,
   Tenant Remedies Act relief)? Note: conciliation court is permissive,
   not exclusive, in current subd. 9 — the draft displaces both forums.
2. **No-subrogation.** Does subd. 3b survive challenge under Minnesota
   contract and insurance law — and does voiding recourse terms in
   tenant contracts (3b(b), 3c) raise any enforceability problem for
   SDR products already written?
3. **Appeal standard.** How should the district-court review of an
   adjudication decision be drafted — substantial-evidence cert-style
   review, UI-model review, or something else? Subd. 3a(g) is the slot.
4. **Found while drafting — fiduciary status.** Existing subd. 2 says a
   deposit is NOT received in a fiduciary capacity (§ 82.55 subd. 26).
   An escrowed deposit IS held in trust — does that trigger broker or
   depository fiduciary duties, and does the conforming amendment need
   to say so expressly?
5. **Found while drafting — transition.** Deposits already held by
   landlords when the act takes effect: 90-day mandatory transmission
   vs. at-renewal, and how the tenant's 1% interest computes across the
   handoff. Subd. 11 treatment above is a placeholder.
