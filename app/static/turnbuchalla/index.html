Findings & Definitions

§·0

Preamble — what the record shows

  • Deposit disputes and serial nonpayment filings consume a third to a half of Minnesota's ~23–26,000 annual eviction filings.
  • Private "deposit insurance" products pay the landlord and then recover from the tenant — creating new debt, not protection.
  • Custodial deposit protection with free independent adjudication has operated nationally in the United Kingdom since 2007, funded off pooled float.
+
§·1

Definitions

Drafting placeholder. "Residential landlord" attaches to the rental relationship, not a municipal license — Minnesota has no statewide rental license. Defined terms to include: Pool, Fund, Administrator, Adjudicator, Covered hardship, Manufactured claim, Clean year.

+

Title I — Deposit Protection

The proven, self-funding core. Amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178. Precedent: UK Tenancy Deposit Protection.

I-1

Custodial escrow

Every residential security deposit is paid into bonded program escrow at lease signing and held there for the tenancy. The landlord never holds, itemizes, or returns the deposit. Statutory 1% interest continues to accrue to the tenant.

Locked design — UK custodial shape.

+
I-2

Independent claims adjudication

Deposit and damage claims are decided by an independent adjudication body on an administrative-law model. Neither the landlord nor the administering carrier decides claims.

+
I-3

Tenant counter-claim lane

Tenants bring deposit claims through the same body; the administrator answers to claimants, not landlords.

+
I-4

No subrogation

A paid claim is not recoverable from the tenant by any party. Applies across all titles — a paid claim ends the matter, forever.

+
I-5

Claims-free dividend

Clean years earn the renter an annual dividend declared from pool surplus — a mutual model, not a guaranteed payment.

+
I-6

Public claims registry

Aggregate per-owner claims data published; accuracy and dispute rules modeled on the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

+
I-7

Manufactured-claim penalties

A claim proven fabricated carries a surcharge plus referral. Symmetric penalty for knowingly-false fraud referrals against tenants; tenant notice and a defense window before any referral transmits.

+
I-8

Funding of Title I

Pooled float above the statutory 1% (≈3% net of escrowed deposits, ≈$23M/year at scale) funds escrow administration and adjudication. Adjudication carries its own budget line.

+
I-9

Deposit-alternative products

Licensed security-deposit replacement products may be offered in Minnesota only if they are no-subrogation, refundable-principal, and report claims through the registry — convert the industry, don't just ban it.

Open drafting decision — new clause under review.

+

Title II — Rent Assurance

The guarantee-fund leg — administered like insurance on the unemployment-insurance precedent. Recommended path: MHFA pilot on subsidized properties first; statewide rollout follows pilot actuarials.

II-1

Covered hardship

The fund pays covered rent to the landlord during verified tenant hardship from a defined trigger list (job loss, medical crisis — documented per UI-style verification) for a defined coverage term.

Open drafting question: trigger list and a 2–6 month term — awaits actuarial review.

+
II-2

No subrogation; forward-rent plan only

A paid claim is not recoverable from the tenant. A repayment plan applies solely to rent accruing after the coverage period — a condition of continued coverage, not of the claim.

+
II-3

Mandatory participation; three-source funding braid

  • Landlord matched share — community-rated base plus a bounded experience surcharge; clean-book years earn a no-claim dividend from surplus.
  • Renter-side share — carried by the MN Renter's Credit as a monthly advance reconciled on the return (the federal advance-premium-credit model); enrollment rides the renter-credit e-file data rail; over-cap renters pay the share directly (~$6/mo), disclosed.
  • Deposit float (Title I) — the ongoing third leg.

Split ratio open pending actuarial (≈$3.25 + $6 + $6 ≈ $15/mo at $1,300 rent).

+
II-4

Year-one ramp and backstop

Coverage phases in as escrow crosses a threshold; or a repayable state advance against future premiums (UI trust-fund precedent), with legislative appropriation backup.

Open drafting decision — one mechanism to be chosen and stated.

+
II-5

Tenant-side controls

Verified triggers, per-lease coverage caps, claims-history pricing at renewal, repayment-plan cooperation.

+
II-6

Pilot authority

The Minnesota Housing Finance Agency may operate Title II as a pilot on publicly-subsidized properties without further appropriation; pilot data constitutes the fiscal note for statewide rollout.

+

Title III — Renter's Belongings Coverage

Included so the structure is visible; shape left open pending actuarial review.

III-1

Contents coverage

Contents coverage (fire / theft / water) rides the same pool as a third line. Opt-in vs. mandatory, cap, and deductible are open pending actuarial. Fire and habitability events coordinate with Title II as a single integrated claim.

+

Title IV — Governance, Home Agency, Review

IV-1

Governing board

Tenant and consumer seats on the governing board — the people the pool serves sit at the table where it is run. Anti-capture by design.

+
IV-2

Home agency

The Fund sits at the Minnesota Housing Finance Agency; adjudication sits at an independent body; licensed-insurer administration is contracted out.

Open drafting decision — proposed answer to the agency-home question.

+
IV-3

Review

Administrative appeal, then district court. Standard of review is an open drafting question.

+
IV-4

Sunset and report

Five-year sunset with a mandatory year-3 report to the housing committees — if it does not work, it ends, and the bill carries its own verdict.

+
IV-5

Single-subject language

Credit-advance mechanics are drafted as machinery for the fund, inside this Act — not as separate tax policy.

+
