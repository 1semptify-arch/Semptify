Working draft — version 0.1 (outline stage)

A bill about housing documentation, so a renter's record counts.

Purpose (our aim). When a renter reports a problem — a repair not made, a deposit withheld, a condition that affects health — their account should carry weight. Today it often doesn't, not because the renter is wrong, but because there is no shared, trusted way to record what happened. This bill aims to set fair, common-sense standards for how housing problems get documented — standards that protect honest renters and honest housing providers alike.

The idea at the center. A documented fact beats a loud argument. If both sides keep the same kind of record — dates, photos, notices, responses — then disputes get resolved faster, good providers can prove they did right, and renters can't be waved away.

What it might contain (still being worked out).

  • Plain-language documentation standards for housing complaints and repairs.
  • How a documented record is treated when a dispute reaches a hearing or court.
  • Reasonable timelines for responses, on both sides.
  • Protections so honest reporting can't be punished.

Open questions (where your voice fits).

  • What should a fair documentation standard actually require — and what is too much to ask of either side?
  • How do small landlords and large operators meet the same standard without crushing the small ones?
  • What does enforcement look like that helps people instead of just punishing?

Status. Outline stage — not filed, not before any legislature. What you see here is the real working text. As it changes, this file changes with it, and the record of every change is public in the open repository.
