What this place is. Semptify is writing a housing bill — and we're doing it where you can watch. This page holds the live draft, the plain-language path a bill travels, and a public board where anyone can ask a question or share an opinion.

How we keep it honest. Every statement here is one of three things, and we say which: a fact with a source, an aim — what we're trying to do — or an opinion. If we get something wrong, we say so on this page, in the open.

Who it's for. Everyone. Renters, housing providers who do right, students learning how a bill becomes a law, neighbors who just want to know what's happening to their homes. This benefits people — it isn't for or against anyone.

The journey a bill takes

In plain language: someone has an idea. It gets written as a draft. Lawmakers sponsor it. A committee studies it and hears the public. If it survives, the whole body votes. If it passes and is signed, it becomes law. Ours is here:

Ask, or weigh in

Received. Your post will appear on this page after a quick review to remove any personal details. Questions wait here, honestly marked, until someone qualified answers them.

No name. No email. Posts appear publicly after a review pass — that's how we stay transparent without tracking anyone.

The review standard. Every post gets a human review before it appears. Review removes only what hurts people: personal details — stripped out, with the rest of the post still going up — threats, harassment, hate (racism, antisemitism, bigotry of any kind), spam, and anything illegal.

Everything else stays. Opinions about the bill — support, criticism, anything in between — are never removed for being wrong or unwelcome. Disagreement is the point; it's how this gets better.

The public board

Every posted question and opinion lives here, with its answer. The people writing the bill — and invited contributors like housing attorneys, legislative staff, and professors — answer what they can, labeled by role.

Passing it on

This lane is also a classroom — from grade schoolers learning how laws are made to interns who may write them someday. Students and volunteers help with research and answers; what they contribute is credited by role, and checked before it posts. If you'd like to help — research, reading, fact-checking — say so on the board above, or see the ways to give time.

Beyond this board: tell your story — longer, anonymized accounts that inform the bill — or reach your legislators through official government channels.
